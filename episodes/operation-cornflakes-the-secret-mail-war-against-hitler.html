About this episode

Turning the postal system into a weapon

By 1945, Allied intelligence organizations were looking for ways to undermine German morale and circulate anti-Nazi material inside the Reich. The U.S. Office of Strategic Services developed Operation Cornflakes, a covert effort to exploit the German postal system.

Forged mail from the air

The plan involved producing propaganda disguised as ordinary German mail, complete with convincing envelopes and postage. Allied aircraft attacked selected mail trains and then dropped bags of forged letters nearby, hoping German postal workers would collect and distribute the material alongside legitimate correspondence.

An unusual psychological operation

The operation depended on confusion, routine, and the credibility of familiar-looking mail. Its overall effect on German morale is difficult to measure, and it came late in the war. Even so, Cornflakes illustrates how intelligence agencies used creative methods beyond traditional battlefield operations, attempting to reach ordinary people through a system they encountered every day.
