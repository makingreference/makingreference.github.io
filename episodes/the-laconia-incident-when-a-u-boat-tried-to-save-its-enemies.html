About this episode

The sinking of RMS Laconia

On September 12, 1942, German submarine U-156, commanded by Werner Hartenstein, torpedoed the British troopship RMS Laconia in the South Atlantic. The ship carried British military personnel and civilians, Polish guards, and approximately 1,800 Italian prisoners of war. As survivors entered the water, Hartenstein realized that many of those aboard were Italian allies of Germany.

A remarkable rescue operation

Hartenstein began rescuing survivors, taking people aboard U-156 and towing lifeboats. Other German U-boats and an Italian submarine joined the effort, while appeals for assistance were broadcast openly. U-156 displayed a Red Cross flag during the rescue. On September 16, an American B-24 bomber attacked the submarine despite its rescue activities, forcing Hartenstein to abandon the operation and submerge after putting survivors back into lifeboats.

The Laconia Order

The rescue continued through other vessels, including French ships sent from Dakar. The incident helped prompt German naval commander Karl Dönitz to issue the Laconia Order, directing U-boat commanders not to undertake rescue efforts for survivors of ships they sank. The episode remains a stark example of the tension between humanitarian action and the brutal realities of submarine warfare.
