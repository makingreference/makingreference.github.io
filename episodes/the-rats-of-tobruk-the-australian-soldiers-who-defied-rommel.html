About this episode

An isolated port under siege

In 1941, the North African port of Tobruk became a vital Allied stronghold after Axis forces advanced across Libya. Australian troops, supported by British and other Allied units, defended the port against forces led by German General Erwin Rommel. Holding Tobruk denied the Axis a valuable harbor and complicated its supply lines.

The defenders refuse to yield

The garrison endured artillery bombardment, air attacks, patrols, and harsh desert conditions. Soldiers conducted aggressive raids and defended fortified positions for months. Axis propaganda used the name “Rats of Tobruk” to belittle the defenders, but the Australians adopted the nickname as a badge of pride.

Why Tobruk mattered

The siege began in April 1941 and lasted until December, although most Australian troops were relieved before its end. The stubborn defense tied down Axis forces and became an important morale symbol across the Commonwealth. Tobruk demonstrated how a determined garrison could influence a wider campaign by holding ground that an advancing army could not afford to ignore.
