About this episode

An island at the center of the Mediterranean

Malta occupied a strategically important position between Italy and North Africa. During World War II, British forces based there could threaten Axis shipping supplying campaigns in Libya and Egypt. That importance made the island a target for repeated Italian and German air attacks.

Surviving the siege

From 1940 through 1942, Malta endured intense bombing, shortages of food and fuel, and mounting pressure on its air defenses. Civilians sheltered underground while RAF pilots and antiaircraft crews fought to keep the island operational. Convoys attempting to deliver supplies faced submarines, aircraft, and surface threats. The arrival of the battered Pedestal convoy in August 1942 helped avert a critical fuel shortage.

A symbol of endurance

In April 1942, King George VI awarded the George Cross to the people of Malta in recognition of their courage. The island remained an important Allied base and contributed to attacks on Axis supply routes. Malta’s survival mattered not only to its residents but also to the wider struggle for control of the Mediterranean.
