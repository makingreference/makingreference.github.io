USS West Virginia - From Pearl Harbor to Victory

About this episode

Sunk at Pearl Harbor

On December 7, 1941, the battleship USS West Virginia was moored along Battleship Row when Japanese aircraft attacked Pearl Harbor. Torpedoes and bombs inflicted devastating damage, and the ship settled into the harbor. Many sailors were killed, but efforts to control flooding helped prevent the vessel from capsizing.

Raised, rebuilt, and returned

West Virginia was later raised and underwent extensive reconstruction. The work transformed the battleship’s appearance and improved its defenses, including updated radar and antiaircraft weapons. In 1944, the ship returned to combat in the Pacific and supported American operations against Japanese positions.

From destruction to victory

During the Battle of Surigao Strait in October 1944, West Virginia used radar-directed gunfire against a Japanese force in one of history’s last battleship engagements. She later supported landings at Iwo Jima and Okinawa and was present in Tokyo Bay for Japan’s surrender in September 1945. Her wartime journey became a powerful story of survival and recovery.
