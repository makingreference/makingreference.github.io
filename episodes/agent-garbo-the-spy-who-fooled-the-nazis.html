About this episode

An imaginary network of spies

Juan Pujol García, a Spaniard determined to oppose Nazi Germany, persuaded German intelligence that he had assembled a network of agents in Britain. The network was fictional. Working with British intelligence under the codename Garbo, he supplied carefully controlled reports that helped build his credibility with the Germans.

The deception behind D-Day

As the Allies prepared to invade occupied France in 1944, Garbo became an important part of Operation Fortitude. His messages reinforced the German belief that the main Allied landings would take place near Pas-de-Calais, across the narrowest part of the English Channel, rather than in Normandy.

The deception continued after June 6

After Allied troops landed in Normandy on D-Day, Garbo continued sending misleading intelligence. His reports helped sustain the German belief that the Normandy landings were a diversion and that a larger attack at Pas-de-Calais was still coming. His work was one element of the wider Allied deception campaign that helped protect the invasion.
