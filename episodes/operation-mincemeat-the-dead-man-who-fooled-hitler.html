About this episode

A deception built around a dead man

In 1943, Allied planners prepared to invade Sicily but wanted German commanders to expect attacks elsewhere in the Mediterranean. British intelligence devised Operation Mincemeat, an extraordinary deception that relied on a body carrying forged military correspondence.

False papers, real consequences

The body was dressed as a Royal Marines officer and given the invented identity of Major William Martin. British personnel arranged for it to wash ashore in Spain, where the documents could reach German intelligence. The letters suggested that Sicily was a diversion and that Allied forces intended to strike Greece and Sardinia.

Helping conceal the Sicily landings

German authorities took the planted information seriously, although historians debate precisely how much it altered their decisions. The Allies landed in Sicily in July 1943 as planned. Mincemeat remains one of the war’s most inventive intelligence operations, showing how convincing details and carefully chosen channels could make a fabricated story appear genuine.
